# 19 — Open questions

These require product, editorial or business input; they are not unresolved merely because implementation detail remains.

1. Which desks and editorial roles exist at launch, and who may approve/fact-check/correct each desk’s work? (The seed holds four provisional desks and eight provisional roles with placeholder capability sets; for example, a copy editor can currently resolve comments and cancel assignments.)
2. What are the canonical public locale policies for English and Bengali, including whether localized slugs may diverge?
3. What correction display policy is required: inline notice, correction history page, or both?
4. Which source types may be ingested automatically (including which specific Telegram channels), and what copyright/terms/robots policy governs retrieval and raw snapshot retention?
5. Which contributor contact fields may migrate and who can view them?
6. What publishing SLA, RPO/RTO, traffic baseline and peak-event assumptions should set capacity targets?
7. Which OIDC provider constraints apply: managed tenancy, self-hosting, region, MFA, directory integration and lifecycle automation?
8. Which distribution channels are launch requirements versus later experiments?
9. What analytics consent, retention and newsroom access policy applies to public readers and internal users (including consent-gating the GA4 tag)?
10. IP-hash retention window and IP-hashing algorithm for AdEvent records: what policy governs how long hashed IPs are kept and how they may be used?
11. What editorial transformation-threshold policy applies to AI-drafted rewrites of ingested source articles before they may be approved — e.g. minimum original reporting/editing, mandatory attribution wording, and any source types excluded from rewrite entirely?
12. Should FactLens support public comments on articles? This carries significant moderation, spam/abuse and legal exposure — many newsrooms have removed on-site comments in favor of social-platform discussion. Decided (21 November 2026): public comments are a future plan, not in scope for the current build. No design work now.
13. Should FactLens support a paid subscription/paywall alongside advertising? This is a monetization/business decision (pricing, payment provider, what content is gated) Decided (21 November 2026): paid subscriptions are a future plan, not in scope for the current build. No design work now.
14. What is the public URL scheme, and how do legacy URLs map to it? `apps/web` serves Bangla at `/` and English under `/en` with codes `bn` and `en`; the docs use `en` and `bn-BD`; cipher-press uses no locale prefix (cookie-based language) and slugs such as `/news/[slug]`. Needs one decision on locale codes, prefix policy and the legacy redirect map (see docs 14 and 16, Phase 0).
15. Is fact-checking part of the product? `apps/web` rule `DR-18` says Fact Lens has no fact-checking product and forbids a fact-check label or verdict feature, while docs 02 and 05 list fact-checking in the newsroom workflow and a claims/evidence graph (Phase 7). Needs one decision; the docs or the web rule must change.
16. Retention windows are unset. `pnpm seed` creates no `retention.*` configuration, because the policies behind questions 4, 9 and 10 are undecided. The desks it seeds are provisional (question 1).
17. Identity provider and sign-in details. Resolved (ADR-046, 18 November 2026): sign-in is managed inside the backend, so no external provider is needed; the newsroom uses its own sign-in page. The real provider (question 7) decides the roles claim name, how MFA is signalled (`amr`), token lifetime, refresh and logout. Desk-scoped roles (a role held for one desk only) are not modeled.
18. Media rules. Which image types beyond JPEG, PNG and WebP (GIF, AVIF, SVG) are needed; the upload size limit (10 MB now); who may upload and who may delete (reporters upload today; deleting needs `media.manage`); what licence wording editors must use; how long an asset with expired rights stays in the library.
19. Confirm the revision rule: a submitted revision is locked, and sending a news item back or revising a published news item opens a new revision ([06](./06-editorial-workflow.md)). The alternative is to reopen the same revision, which loses the record of what was reviewed.
20. Session length. Resolved (ADR-046): an editor's sign-in lasts 8 hours, with 15-minute access tokens refreshed silently. Access tokens are the session (no refresh yet). Is a sign-in per token lifetime acceptable to editors, or is silent refresh a launch requirement?
21. Untranslated categories and tags. Decided: omit. Something with no name in a language is left out of that language's menu, news item page and lists; languages are never mixed. Falling back to another language's name was considered and rejected. Still open: should the menu have sub-menus (the column exists, nothing uses it), and should tags be capped at 30 per news item as now?
22. Read next rules. Are the proposed selection rules and weights in [23](./23-read-next.md) (editor's picks, then shared topics 3, regions 2, sections 1 with a recency boost, then the newest of the section) acceptable as a first version, to be tuned on real content? Resolved: yes. The weights and rules in [23](./23-read-next.md) section 3 are the first version, to be tuned on real content.
23. Topics. The public site has a managed topic list (what or who, one Latin address, a name per language, a page at `/topic/<slug>`), and the plan lists Topic as an entity that is not built. Are topics the existing tags, or their own table? Read next and the topic page both depend on it. Resolved: the existing tags are the topic signal for the first version (146, from cipher-press). A separate topic table waits until the topic page is approved.
24. Opinion in Read next. The public site's lists keep opinion out of news, but its Read next mockup shows an opinion piece. May opinion appear in Read next, and under what condition (the proposal: at most one, and only one that shares a topic)? Resolved: opinion may appear in Read next, at most one, and only one that shares a topic with the article.
25. Reading time override. Does the first version of [24](./24-reading-time.md) ship with the editor's override (blank means calculated), or calculated only? The override is one nullable column and easy to add later. Resolved: the first version ships with the reading-time override (blank means calculated).
26. Trending search terms. Does the "Trending" row of the search dialog start from an editorial list (up to 6 terms per language, set in the newsroom), with terms computed from searches added later and the editorial terms pinned first, as proposed in [26](./26-search-and-trending.md)? Resolved: trending starts from the editorial list (up to 6 terms per language, pinned first); computed terms come later.
27. Bangla search. Are the `simple` text-search configuration plus trigram matching on titles enough for the first version, or must stemming or a Bangla-specific dictionary be in place before launch? PostgreSQL has no Bangla stemmer. Resolved: `simple` plus trigram matching on titles is enough for the first version. A Bangla dictionary is not required before launch; revisit on measured search quality.
28. Search queries kept. May search queries be kept, anonymously and as counts only, to improve search ("searches with no results") and to compute trending terms, and for how long? Any query that looks like personal data would not be counted. Resolved: search queries are not kept in the first version. The no-results signal and computed trending wait for a separate privacy review.

Resolved: advertising is confirmed part of the Phase 1 launch scope, carrying forward CipherPress's current Advertiser/Campaign/Placement/Ad/Event design as-is (see [product and platform scope](./02-product-and-platform-scope.md) and [domain model](./05-domain-model.md)).

Resolved: Telegram channels are a named ingestion source type, using the Bot API (see [ingestion and automation](./08-ingestion-and-automation.md)); which specific channels remains covered by open question 4.

29. Card renders with identical bytes. Each card output is a media asset whose checksum is unique in the database, so two cards that render to the same bytes (for example the same photo, template and text requested twice) would collide and the second render would fail. Resolved: card outputs are exempt from the unique checksum index, so identical renders are allowed; uploads still deduplicate by checksum and never match a card output ([ADR-036](./17-architecture-decisions.md)).

30. HTTP status of the unavailable page. Resolved (17 November 2026): with the API down, page requests get HTTP 503, retry-after 30 and the localized page, from a cached readiness check in the middleware (once every 10 seconds per process). Checked by hand: 503 with the API unreachable, 200 in mock mode.
